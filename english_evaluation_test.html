The Urban Farming Revolution

In downtown Chicago, surrounded by towering concrete skyscrapers and noisy morning traffic, Mark Stevens operates an extraordinary agricultural project: a thriving 10,000-square-foot rooftop farm. Three years ago, Mark had been managing a conventional corporate office job when he realized how disconnected urban residents had become from their food sources. Determined to make a change, he quit his corporate career, gathered funding from local environmental investors, and leased the roof of an old industrial warehouse.

Today, Mark's farm produces hundreds of pounds of organic leafy greens, heirloom tomatoes, and culinary herbs every week. Instead of utilizing traditional soil, the rooftop uses advanced hydroponic and drip-irrigation systems that conserve up to 90 percent more water than conventional rural farms. Furthermore, because the food is harvested and delivered directly to nearby neighborhood markets and restaurants on the same day, transport emissions are virtually eliminated.

Beyond growing fresh produce, the initiative actively serves the local community. Every Saturday morning, Mark hosts free educational workshops for local high school students, teaching them about sustainable urban ecology and modern agricultural technology. Although managing weather unpredictability and winter freezing remains a continuous challenge, Mark plans to expand his rooftop concept to three additional industrial buildings across the city by next spring.

1. What was Mark doing before he established the rooftop farm?

2. How does Mark's rooftop irrigation system compare to traditional farming?

3. Why are transport emissions virtually eliminated in Mark's business model?

4. What community activity takes place at the farm every Saturday?

5. What future plan does Mark have for his urban farm project?

On their second morning, the group began ascending the steep trail toward Glacier Point. They had been hiking for almost three hours when they spotted a mother black bear and her two cubs near a pine grove. Fortunately, the team had been practicing safety protocol during their training sessions, so they remained calm and allowed the bear family to pass peacefully.

By late afternoon, the clouds cleared, revealing a panoramic view of Half Dome sunset. David noted that although his muscles were aching from the climb, the experience was the highlight of his year.
